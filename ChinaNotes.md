# China economy

The tradeoff is between policies that boost consumption and a continuation of supply side push / industrial policies. 
It's easy to see how and why a transition to consumption is necessary and helpful.  But supply side policies have worked well, so are a low cost path for lower-level policymakers, e.g. local governments, but even PBOC/MOF.  This is compounded by the fact that the industrial sector in China has been so efficient and effective, has taken market share overseas and become 'industrial superstars', which makes the problem worse from a political economy standpoint given that is viewed as the 'easy option'.

From top leadership perspective, there is a tendency to down-weight growth. Growth is no longer the key consideration, but rather from a global perspective, need to think about China's role in an increasingly polarized world. National security has become more important, as is securing supply chains, self reliance, avoid bottlenecks etc. All of these require continued push in industrial policies.

Policymakres understand that growth and consumption is relevant, but in the short-term these represent a tradeoff versus continued push in industrial policies. In the 'old days' when the external environment was more friendly, this may be viewed as an opportunity to push for growth and household income. But the current external environment makes that less of a priority, so resources may only start to shift towards these areas when the absence of support starts to bump up against the 'bottom line'. We saw this in the reopening, as well as Sept 2024 policies. It's not about the stock market, or household wealth etc.
